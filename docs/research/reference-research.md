# Light Raiders — Reference Research Brief

Compiled 2026-07-02 from five research reports (Arc Raiders systems, top-down extraction design, Unity netcode, asset ecosystem, scope reality). Source data reflects the live landscape as of mid-2026.

---

## 1. TLDR

- **No solo-dev PvP extraction shooter has ever shipped and survived.** The proven lane is single-player/co-op PvE first: ZERO Sievert (solo dev) made $1.38M in week one; even its co-op sequel required founding a whole new studio of ex-Splash Damage veterans.
- **PvP extraction has a consistent death spiral:** cheaters/bugs at launch → population drops below matchmaking viability within weeks → seasonal content can't reverse it → shutdown in ~12–18 months (The Cycle: Frontier, Spectre Divide, Steel Hunters; Marauders now at 2–17 CCU; top-down PvP Hell is Others peaked at 937 players ever).
- **Netcode decision hinges on prediction, not player count:** NGO is the best-tutorialized co-op path (Lethal Company runs on it) but has no built-in client-side prediction or lag compensation; FishNet is the only free stack shipping CSP free + lag compensation in cheap Pro ($10 + $2/mo, or $60 lifetime); Photon Fusion has everything built in but perpetual CCU opex (100 CCU free tier). 12–20 players/raid is comfortably inside every option's envelope.
- **Write server-authoritative gameplay from day one** (never trust the client for damage/loot/inventory), ship co-op on player-host + Unity Relay for ~$0 (free ≤50 avg CCU), and migrate to dedicated servers later — Edgegap-style orchestration runs ~$0.02–0.07 per server-hour. Note: Unity Multiplay shut down March 31, 2026 and Hathora May 5, 2026.
- **Vision fairness is the #1 top-down complaint:** player vision, enemy detection range, rendered info, and roof-cutaway range must be one consistent system — ZERO Sievert's "sniper hidden behind a single pixel of foliage" memes and Project Zomboid's cutaway-extension mod are both symptoms of mismatch. Rule of thumb: map/terrain stays visible, entities are strictly LOS-gated.
- **Map formula that works:** fixed handcrafted landmarks + shuffled connective tissue; proc-gen alone reads as "a null value" (PC Gamer on ZERO Sievert). ZS2's answer to repetition is dynamic in-raid events (weather, faction conflicts), not more proc-gen.
- **Arc Raiders raid template worth copying:** max 3-player squads, ~15–21 players/map, 30-min raids, loud "public" extraction (elevator + alarm) vs. purchasable silent extraction (Raider Hatch, ~9,000 Coins), Safe Pocket (1–3 slots, no weapons) instead of insurance, and a free unlimited loadout as the death floor.
- **Death-penalty tunability sells:** ZERO Sievert's difficulty ladder (keep gear → 15–25% durability loss → lose everything → permadeath) is repeatedly credited for approachability; directional audio is the fair answer to off-screen threats.
- **Asset budget is small:** a viable vertical slice costs $0–50 (Quaternius/KayKit/Kenney CC0), the realistic sweet spot is ~$100–250 (Synty Humble bundle $30 + Sci-Fi Space $149.99 for interiors + Animation Base Locomotion $69.99 + one VFX pack), full buy-out ~$600–1,200. Synty characters ship with **no animations**, and top-down needs roof-cutaway shaders you write yourself.
- **Timeline reality:** solo single-player extraction ≈ 2–4 years to Early Access; 3-person multiplayer FPS ≈ 7 years part-time (BattleBit); launch EA only when the loop is already fun — over-performers averaged 214 days on Steam pre-release and 91% first-week review scores vs 411 days / 67% for underperformers.

## 2. Arc Raiders systems reference

Embark Studios, released Oct 30, 2025. Numbers reflect the live game as of mid-2026 (patch ~1.33); balance values have shifted across patches.

### 2.1 Raid structure

- **Squads:** solo, duo, or trio — max 3. Matchmaking prioritizes solos-vs-solos and squads-vs-squads ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/arc-raiders-squad-size-and-matchmaking-how-trios-shape-every-raid/), [fandomwire](https://fandomwire.com/arc-raiders-squad-size-explained-can-you-play-solo-at-launch/)).
- **Players per map:** ~15–21 total (~5–7 squads), density variable; backfill spawning continues up to ~16 minutes into the raid ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/arc-raiders-player-counts-per-map-squads-matchmaking-and-raids/), [gamerblurb](https://gamerblurb.com/articles/arc-raiders-how-many-players-per-map)).
- **Raid duration:** 30 min standard; Spaceport and Blue Gate stretch to 30–40 min under certain map conditions; Practice Range allows 2 hours ([arcraiders.wiki/Maps](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Maps)).
- **Maps (6 as of mid-2026):** Dam Battlegrounds (level 2), Buried City (lvl 3, 8 rounds played), The Spaceport (lvl 3, 12 rounds), The Blue Gate (lvl 4, 18 rounds), Stella Montis (lvl 4, 24 rounds — endgame moon/bunker zone, highest-value loot, added Dec 2025), Riven Tides (lvl 5, 20 rounds — added 2026) ([arcraiders.wiki/Maps](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Maps), [arcraidershub](https://arcraidershub.com/maps)).
- **Map sizes:** ~0.6–1.8 km² playable; Spaceport largest at ~1.6 × 1.4 km (~2.24 km²); Dam Battlegrounds ~0.8–1.2 km²; Buried City ~1.2–1.6 km²; Stella Montis smallest. Fan-measured, treat as approximate ([arcraidersmap.us](https://arcraidersmap.us/arc-raiders-map-size-comparison/)).
- **Map conditions/events** rotate hourly per map: Night Raid, Cold Snap, Hurricane, Electromagnetic Storm, Close Scrutiny, Locked Gate, Launch Tower Loot, Hidden Bunker, Matriarch/Harvester spawns ([arcraidershub events](https://arcraidershub.com/events), [ssegold](https://www.ssegold.com/arc-raiders-event-timers-guide)).

### 2.2 Extraction mechanics

- **Four extraction types:** cargo elevators, metro stations, airshafts, Raider Hatches ([arcraiders.wiki/Extraction_Points](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Extraction_Points)).
- **Normal (elevator):** call at external panel → loud alarm broadcasts your position to ARC machines and hostile Raiders → wait → enter and activate internal terminal → departs shortly after activation, auto-leaves once someone is inside ([games.gg](https://games.gg/arc-raiders/guides/how-to-extract-in-arc-raiders/), [allthings.how](https://allthings.how/arc-raiders-extraction-elevators-hatches-and-timers-explained/), [dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/wikis/arc-raiders/how-to-extract/)). Exact elevator arrival/ride durations in seconds are not documented anywhere.
- **Shutdown timers:** standard extracts display a countdown to terminal deactivation; interacting just before shutdown grants "overtime" — escape after expiry ([arcraiders.wiki/Extraction_Points](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Extraction_Points)).
- **Raider Hatch (silent alternate):** consumes a single-use key (~9,000 Coins from Shani, level-gated, daily purchase limit with price escalation). No alarm. Open ~10–15 s; any Raider can enter but only one at a time; **no shutdown timer**, so it works for late arrivals ([arcraiders.wiki/Extraction_Points](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Extraction_Points), [thegamer](https://www.thegamer.com/arc-raiders-extract-raider-hatch-raider-hatch-key-extraction-tips-guide-how-to/), [gamerant](https://gamerant.com/arc-raiders-how-to-extract-raider-hatch/)). Standard advice: keep the key in the Safe Pocket so it survives death ([leprestore](https://leprestore.com/guides/arc-raiders/escape-routes-safe-exits/)).
- **Downed players can still interact with extraction terminals** and crawl out ([arcraiders.wiki/Extraction_Points](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Extraction_Points)). Firefights concentrate around elevators/metro exits late in the raid as timers wind down ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/arc-raiders-player-counts-per-map-squads-matchmaking-and-raids/)).

### 2.3 Death & loot rules

- **Full elimination loses:** equipped weapons, backpack and body slots, all consumables; corpse gear is lootable by players and ARC machines ([speranzaintel](https://speranzaintel.com/guides/article/?slug=how-death-works)).
- **Safe Pocket:** items auto-return to stash on death. Base 1 slot; Looting Mk.2 augment → 2; certain Mk.3/Epic augments → 3. Weapons excluded (exception: Looting Mk.3 "Safekeeper" allows any item) ([gamerant](https://gamerant.com/arc-raiders-prevent-item-loss-on-death-safe-pockets/), [arcraiders.wiki/Augments](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Augments)).
- **Downed vs eliminated:** downed squadmates are revivable (defibrillator or teammate revive); no item loss unless fully eliminated ([speranzaintel](https://speranzaintel.com/guides/article/?slug=how-death-works)).
- **No insurance system.** The substitute is the **Free Loadout**: unlimited, no-cooldown, zero-cost random kit — one of Kettle I / Rattler I / Stitcher I / Ferro I with one ammo stack, Light Shield, meds, a gadget. Caveat: free loadouts have **no safe pocket** ([steamcommunity](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1808500/discussions/0/669474225528125814/), [pcgamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/third-person-shooter/arc-raiders-free-loadout/), [arcraiders.wiki/Free_Loadout](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Free_Loadout)).

### 2.4 Player kit: health, shields, augments, healing

- **Health:** flat 100 HP for everyone, never increases with progression; no passive regen (some augments add trickle regen) ([nerdschalk](https://nerdschalk.com/arc-raiders-player-health-hp-shields-system-explained/)).
- **Shields = damage mitigation, not bonus HP** — absorb a percentage of incoming damage while charged ([arcraiders.wiki/Shields](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Shields)):
  - Light: 40 charge, 40% mitigation, no movement penalty, weight 5.0, fits all augments
  - Medium: 70 charge, 42.5% mitigation, 5% movement penalty, weight 7.0
  - Heavy: 80 charge, 52.5% mitigation, 15% movement penalty, weight 9.0, only Combat/Tactical Mk.2–3 augments
  - Durability: a full-durability shield depletes its charge ~5 times before breaking.
- **Augments** (backpack system): one equipped device defines backpack slots, weight cap, quick-use slots, safe-pocket count, shield compatibility, and a unique perk; cannot swap mid-raid; lose 30 durability per knockout ([arcraiders.wiki/Augments](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Augments), [gamesradar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/third-person-shooter/arc-raiders-augments/)). Three families across Mk.1–3:
  - **Combat** (16–20 slots, weight 45–65): best shield access, grenade slots; Mk.2/3 add passive regen (1–2 HP/5s, pauses 30s after damage); Mk.3 Flanking: 33% faster pistol/hand-cannon swap.
  - **Looting** (18–24 slots, weight 50–80): biggest bags, usually Light-shield-only; perks: auto-eject Ticks, auto-adrenaline on shield break, regen to 75% while downed (Survivor), any-item safe pocket (Safekeeper).
  - **Tactical** (15–20 slots, weight 40–65): most quick-use slots (5), smallest bags; perks: auto-smoke on shield break, shield-recharge-while-sprinting, healing cloud on revive, integrated defibrillator (Revival).
- **Healing/consumables** ([arcraiders.wiki/Healing](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Healing), [allthings.how](https://allthings.how/arc-raiders-healing-guide-every-medical-item-and-how-to-use-them/)): 15 health items — Bandage 2 HP/s ×10s, Herbal Bandage 3.5/s ×10s, Sterilized Bandage 5/s ×10s (all 1.5s use); Vita Shot +50 HP instant (4s use); Vita Spray 10 HP/s beam, 150 capacity, heals allies; Defibrillator revives at 50 HP (1.5s). Shield rechargers from ARC Powercell (2 charge/s) to Surge Shield Recharger (+50 instant). Nine stamina items; Adrenaline Shot: 5/s + 100 bonus stamina for 10s.

### 2.5 Weapons

- **Scale:** ~84 weapons in 9–10 classes: Assault Rifles (Kettle, Rattler, Arpeggio, Tempest, Bettina), Battle Rifles (Ferro, Renegade, Aphelion), SMGs (Stitcher, Canto, Bobcat), Shotguns (Il Toro, Vulcano, Dolabra), Pistols (Hairpin, Burletta, Venator), Hand Cannons (Anvil), LMGs (Torrente), Snipers (Osprey, Jupiter), Specials (Rascal, Hullcracker, Equalizer) ([arcraiders.wiki/Weapons](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Weapons), [arcraidermap](https://arcraidermap.com/weapons)).
- **Tiers & rarity:** Roman numerals I–IV are permanent Gunsmith upgrades (most weapons take 3; upgrading restores appearance). Rarity Common→Legendary; Legendary weapons cannot be upgraded ([arcraiders.wiki/Weapons](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Weapons)).
- **ARC-tech energy weapons** (Energy Clip ammo): Aphelion (Legendary battle rifle, 2-round burst, 25 dmg), Dolabra (Legendary semi-auto energy shotgun, 50/40 dmg, wide-cone or medium-range beams, ~2-shots players, no mod slots), Jupiter (bolt-action energy sniper, 60 dmg, no mods), Equalizer (full-auto special, 8 dmg @ 33.3 rps) ([arcraiders.wiki/Weapons](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Weapons), [scufgaming](https://www.scufgaming.com/us/en/gaming/games/arc-raiders/arc-raiders-weapons-tier-list/)).
- **Crafting from enemy parts:** energy weapons need Gunsmith Lv3 + boss-tier ARC drops — Dolabra requires Magnetic Accelerators (Queens/Matriarchs/Stella Montis) and Vaporizer Regulators (Vaporizer kills during Close Scrutiny); its blueprint drops from ARC Assessor dropship containers during Close Scrutiny. Aphelion's blueprint: Stella Montis containers or Matriarch kills. Jupiter/Equalizer are craft-only, never trader-purchasable ([arcraidershub Dolabra guide](https://arcraidershub.com/guides/dolabra-blueprint-guide), [arcstatus blueprints](https://arcstatus.com/arc-raiders-blueprints)).
- **Dedicated anti-ARC weapon:** Hullcracker — pump-action 40mm grenade launcher whose rounds only detonate on ARC machines (useless in PvP); guaranteed blueprint from Tian Wen's quest "The Major's Footlocker"; requires Gunsmith Lv3 ([allthings.how](https://allthings.how/arc-raiders-hullcracker-blueprint-what-it-unlocks-and-how-to-craft-it/), [boostmatch](https://boostmatch.gg/blog/arc-raiders/articles/hullcracker-arc-raiders-guide)).

### 2.6 Progression

- **Skill tree:** 3 branches — Mobility (traversal: Nimble Climber, Vaults on Vaults on Vaults @36 pts), Survival (stealth/loot: Agile Croucher, In-round Crafting), Conditioning (resilience/melee: Blast-Born, Effortless Swing, Survivor's Stamina @15 pts). ~15–17 skills per branch, 1–5 points each; 1 point per level, 76 base points total (branch caps ~18/37/21 per one guide); +up to 5 per completed Expedition. Full respec: 2,000 Coins per point ([arcraiders.wiki/Skills](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Skills), [arcraidershub](https://arcraidershub.com/guides/recommended-skill-tree-guide)).
- **Quests:** issued by Speranza traders — Shani (18 quests, sells hatch keys), Celeste (12), Tian Wen (7, gunsmith), Apollo (6), Lance (4), plus nomadic envoy Ermal (patch 1.29.0). Mostly fetch/visit/kill-ARC; rewards include blueprints and gear ([arcraiders.gg/traders](https://arcraiders.gg/traders/), [arcraidershub quests](https://arcraidershub.com/quests), [arcraiders.com](https://arcraiders.com/news/meet-the-traders-blog)).
- **Trials (weekly ranked endgame, unlocks lvl 15):** 5 weekly challenges, best score per challenge counts; **points only register if you extract**. Weekly gates at 1,000/2,500/4,000 points award random Uncommon/Rare/Epic items or blueprints; up to 15 loot crates/week via star ratings; 100-person divisions; season-end cosmetics by rank ([arcraiders.wiki/Trials](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Trials), [leprestore](https://leprestore.com/guides/arc-raiders/trials-explained/), [arcraiders.com](https://arcraiders.com/news/quick-guide-to-trials-season-3)).
- **Expeditions (prestige, unlocks lvl 20, ~2-month cycle):** 6-stage Preparation Phase (commit materials to build a caravan) then a 1–2 week Expedition Window. Resets level, skills, inventory, stash upgrades, Raider Decks, workshop progress; keeps maps, cosmetics, blueprints, Raider Tokens, Coins, Cred, merits, achievements. Rewards: permanent stash space/skill points/cosmetics + stacking temporary buffs (+10% repair, +5% XP, +6% Scrappy materials per completion, stack ×3, lost if a window is missed). Bonus skill points by in-window damage: 1/2/3/4/5 pts at 5k/10k/30k/50k/100k ([arcraiders.wiki/Expedition](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Expedition), [arcraiders.com](https://arcraiders.com/news/the-expedition-project-is-departing-soon)).
- **Workshop:** stations — Gunsmith, Refiner (raw parts → components, e.g., Metal+Rubber → Mechanical Components), Medical Lab, Gear Bench, Explosives Station, plus Scrappy (auto-scavenging rooster drone, 5 levels: Lv2 costs a Dog Collar; Lv5 "Master Hoarder" costs 12 Mushrooms + 12 Apricots + 3 Very Comfortable Pillows). Combat stations max at Lv3; upgrades gate blueprints (Gunsmith Lv3: 3 Rusted Gears, 5 Advanced Mechanical Components, 4 Sentinel Firing Cores) ([skycoach](https://skycoach.gg/blog/arc-raiders/articles/workshop-upgrade-guide), [games.gg](https://games.gg/arc-raiders/guides/arc-raiders-workshop-upgrade-guide/), [arcraiders.wiki/Workshop](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/Workshop)).
- **Crafting/recycling loop:** loot → extract → recycle from inventory for full material yield in town; scrapping mid-raid yields only 50%. Blueprints are permanent unlocks. Recycling generally beats selling; diamond-marked "trinkets" exist purely to sell for Coins. ARC Alloy comes from recycling ARC parts (ARC Circuitry, Bastion/Bombardier Cells, Rocketeer Drivers, Sentinel Firing Cores) ([thegamer](https://www.thegamer.com/arc-raiders-materials-safe-recycle-salvage-stash-crafting-quest-guide/), [pcgamesn](https://www.pcgamesn.com/arc-raiders/recycle), [gamerant](https://gamerant.com/arc-raiders-all-resources-materials-loot-guide/)).

### 2.7 Enemy taxonomy (ARC machines)

**Universal AI model** ([arcraiders.wiki/ARC](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/ARC)): three scanner-color alert states — **Blue** (passive patrol), **Yellow** (alerted/investigating), **Red** (target acquired/combat). Three armor surfaces: matte-white unarmored (all weapons work), shiny dark-gray armored (needs penetration or deflects), bright-yellow weak points (priority damage).

**Small/light:**
- **Tick** (ground): spider-like indoor ambusher, leaps and latches for continuous damage; heard before seen. Drops Tick Pod, Flex Rubber.
- **Pop** (ground): ground-level ambush self-detonator. Drops Pop Trigger, Crude Explosives.
- **Snitch** (flying): non-lethal scout that loudly alerts nearby ARC. Drops Snitch Scanner, Sensors.
- **Spotter** (flying): recon/target-designation, coordinates other units. Drops Spotter Relay.
- **Wasp** (flying): basic patrol drone, projectile fire; thruster weak point. Drops Light Ammo, Wasp Driver.

**Medium:**
- **Hornet** (flying): upgunned Wasp, coordinated attacks, audio attack tell; unarmored rear thrusters. Drops Hornet Driver.
- **Firefly** (flying): thermal/incendiary attacker; yellow body-canister weak point. Drops Firefly Burner.
- **Shredder** (ground): aggressive close-range melee with rotating blades, telegraphed attacks; thruster weak point; Stella Montis spawner. Drops Shredder Gyro.
- **Surveyor** (ground): laser-armed, flees at high speed when engaged (loot-piñata behavior), exposed while firing; can summon Assessor dropships. Drops Surveyor Vault, Advanced Powercells.
- **Comet / Fireball** (ground): defensive self-detonating fire-burst units; Fireball's white core exposes when opened. Drop Igniter/Burner parts.
- **Turret** (stationary): sustained-beam emplacement.

**Large/heavy:**
- **Sentinel** (large stationary): long-range sniper turret in open areas, solid red beam when aggroed; yellow arm-canister weak point. Drops Sentinel Firing Core, Heavy Ammo.
- **Bombardier** (ground, large): artillery barrage, escorted by Wasps/Hornets; yellow leg joints + rear canister. Drops Bombardier Cell, Launcher Ammo.
- **Bastion** (ground, massive): slow heavy walker with fast-turning high-power turret; yellow leg joints + rear canister. Drops Bastion Cell.
- **Leaper** (ground, enormous): multi-limb jumping assault, aggressive pursuit, escorted; eye + yellow leg joints. Drops Leaper Pulse Unit.
- **Rocketeer** (enormous, thruster-mobile): supersized Hornet firing rockets that can strip shield+health in one hit; weak points at thrusters/scanner/silver canister. Drops Rocketeer Driver.
- **Vaporizer** (enormous): energy laser plus Matriarch-style energy shield that blocks thrown objects; Close Scrutiny spawn. Drops Vaporizer Regulator, Energy Clips.
- **ARC Turbine** (enormous): cluster lightning missiles and minefields; vulnerable spinning component only while landed. Drops Turbine Compressor.

**Bosses:** **Queen** (colossal walker: homing missiles, flashbangs, gas mines, calls ARC reinforcements; red glowing core + yellow leg markings) and **Matriarch** (event variant with cooldown-cycled energy shield; same core weak points). Both drop Complex Gun Parts, Advanced Components, Queen/Matriarch Reactors ([arcraiders.wiki/ARC](https://arcraiders.wiki/wiki/ARC), [thegamer](https://www.thegamer.com/arc-raiders-enemy-types-defeat-guide/), [epiccarry](https://epiccarry.com/blogs/arc-raiders-enemy-guide/)). Event-spawned **Harvester** and **Assessor dropships** (red-beam loot platforms called by Surveyors) appear on rotating map-condition timers ([arcraidershub events](https://arcraidershub.com/events)).

**Community tiering:** Low = Wasp/Hornet/Snitch/Tick/Pop/Fireball/Turret; Medium = Sentinel/Surveyor/Shredder/Spotter; High = Rocketeer/Bastion/Bombardier/Leaper; Boss = Queen/Matriarch ([lootbar](https://www.lootbar.com/blog/en/arc-raiders-combat-and-enemy-guide.html), [ssegold](https://www.ssegold.com/arc-raiders-all-enemy-types-ranked)).

## 3. Top-down extraction design lessons (ZERO Sievert et al.)

### 3.1 ZERO Sievert — the proof case

Solo dev (Luca Carbonera), GameMaker; EA Nov 2022, 1.0 Oct 2024. Pitch: "S.T.A.L.K.E.R. setting + Tarkov loop, single-player, top-down pixel art" ([gamemaker.io blog](https://gamemaker.io/en/blog/zero-seivert-game-development), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/zero-sievert-steam-pc-game-extraction-shooter-tarkov-1849955431)).

**Commercial facts:** $1,380,963 revenue week one; $3.1M by month 2; 184,862 units by Jan 2023; launched with 277,966 wishlists; 600,000+ copies by 2025; Metascore 81 ([howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/01/18/how-a-pixel-art-shooter-achieved-a-million-dollar-launch-the-zero-sievert-story/), [wasdland](https://www.wasdland.com/games/extraction-shooter/), [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/zero-sievert/critic-reviews/)). Discovery engines: demo before Kickstarter + Steam Next Fest (68,564 wishlists) + streamers (Splattercat alone drove 3,000+ wishlists converting at ~22%).

**Raid loop:** pick zone from bunker hub → load in with chosen gear → loot, fight bandits/mutants, do quests → reach extraction → bank loot. Die and you lose what you carried (difficulty-dependent). No PvP; tension = randomized enemy spawns + loot-vs-survival calculation + gear-loss risk ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1782120/ZERO_Sievert/), [Finger Guns](https://fingerguns.net/features/2022/11/11/zero-sievert-early-access-review-pc-not-the-end-of-the-world/)). No hard raid timer — pressure from hunger/radiation/weight and night cycles. Trader quests structure raids and push players to new POIs; Shacknews (9/10) credits quests with "mitigating repetition through varied objectives" ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/141916/zero-sievert-review-score)). Skill points accrue from in-raid actions; weapon modding (scopes, muzzles, mags) is a major retention system.

**Map structure — the key pattern:** ~5–6 handcraft-anchored procedural maps unlocked in sequence (Forest → Industrial Area → Swamp → Sewers → Mall…). **Fixed landmarks, shuffled connective tissue:** Industrial Area always spawns you along the southern railway, always has an oil factory, two container yards, a port NE, train depot N — only arrangement varies ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/guides/zero-sievert-industrial-area-map-and-locations-guide), [Fandom](https://zero-sievert.fandom.com/wiki/Industrial_Area)). Extraction points semi-randomized per raid (Mall: usually one on the southern edge, one on any edge, plus a hidden underground storage-room extract) ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/guides/zero-sievert-mall-map-and-locations-guide)). Verdict split: "explorable without being overwhelming" ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/141916/zero-sievert-review-score)) vs "a null value — adds nothing, takes nothing away" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/zero-sievert-review/), [Steam community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1782120/discussions/0/5239403117942664257/)). **Lesson: proc-gen buys "check your corners again" paranoia, not real variety — budget accordingly.**

**Hideout/stash:** personal bunker with stash, workbench, and buildable module slots; workbench crafts provisions/meds/ammo/repair kits and repairs/mods weapons and armor; traders + quest givers live in the same hub, making it the meta-progression anchor ([Fandom Hideout](https://zero-sievert.fandom.com/wiki/Hideout), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1782120/ZERO_Sievert/)).

**Death rules — tunable, and that's the point:** default = respawn at bunker, keep equipped gear, lose only that raid's loot. Presets scale: Survivor = 15% gear durability loss + used consumables gone; Hunter = 25% durability + 33% of remaining consumables; Hardcore = lose everything; Permadeath = run over. Fully custom difficulty exists ([Fandom Difficulty Settings](https://zero-sievert.fandom.com/wiki/Difficulty_Settings), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1782120/discussions/0/3387294407411187531/)). No native insurance; a popular mod adds "pay 10% of carried value per raid" insurance ([Nexus](https://www.nexusmods.com/zerosievert/mods/132)). Criticized: difficulty can't change mid-playthrough without restart ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/141916/zero-sievert-review-score)).

**Why it works single-player:** keeps Tarkov's stakes (gear loss, scarcity, lethal TTK) while removing multiplayer's unfairness (cheaters, no pause, sweat). Fan quote: "I get to play it at my own pace… It's also hard enough to still be fun" ([howtomarketagame.com](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/01/18/how-a-pixel-art-shooter-achieved-a-million-dollar-launch-the-zero-sievert-story/)). AI + LOS substitute for player threat — "the extraction loop in its purest mechanical form, stripped of multiplayer entirely" ([iflpvp](https://iflpvp.com/best-extraction-shooters-2026/)); "the perfect entry point to the extraction genre" (Multiplayer.it via [Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/zero-sievert/critic-reviews/)).

**Criticisms (avoid these):**
- **Off-screen death / vision unfairness:** memes about dying to a sniper "hidden behind a single pixel of foliage" ([GameMaker interview](https://gamemaker.io/en/blog/zero-sievert-interview)); enemy spotting range exceeds player rendered vision, so "combat feels like fitting the enemy into a convenient corner of the screen"; vision cone sometimes draws through walls ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/ZERO%20Sievert)).
- **Aiming feel:** "floaty" cursor; recoil makes follow-ups hard while "enemies track you with inhuman accuracy" ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/141916/zero-sievert-review-score)); a shooting rework drew "aiming is impossible" threads ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1782120/discussions/0/4032473829613536024/)).
- **Repetitive quests:** "kill a specific named enemy 15 times = visit one map 15 times" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/roguelike/zero-sievert-review/)).
- **Proc-gen sameness:** "same landmarks shuffled in a different order; some maps not random at all" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1782120/discussions/0/4335356121964610676/)).
- **Night/light balance:** flashlights/NVG "barely help in the dark, ensuring enemies always get the drop on you"; using a torch throws away enemies' reduced night vision ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1782120/discussions/0/3544924290428773655/), [Nexus fix mod](https://www.nexusmods.com/zerosievert/mods/82)).
- Weak onboarding/presentation ([Metacritic](https://www.metacritic.com/game/zero-sievert/critic-reviews/), [TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/previews/zero-sievert-is-unrefined-but-atmospheric-survival-experience)).

**ZERO Sievert 2 (announced Apr 2026)** — top-down PvE extraction sequel by Underdog (ex-Splash Damage, founded 2025 by Giuseppe Navarria and Antoine Fion), publishers Modern Wolf + CABO ([Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/article/2026/4/30/news/149766-zero-sievert-2-is-a-top-down-pve-extraction-shooter-coming-to-early-access-developed-by-former-splash-damage-vets-screens-trailer/)). Additions: 4-player online co-op (each Hunter keeps own gear/XP/quests in a shared Hunt); dynamic weather that changes a run (fog, blizzards cutting visibility, acid rain as a timer, faction-conflict events blocking routes); Relics = anomaly artifacts with passive bonuses/active abilities that destabilize with use; 4 skill paths (Combat, Survival, Recon, Ingenuity) + per-weapon and per-map Mastery; 30+ weapons with maintenance; same "procedural layout + fixed landmarks" formula; EA launches with 2 maps, ~8-month EA, updates every 2 months ([Steam ZS2](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4210060/ZERO_Sievert_2/), [AllKeyShop](https://www.allkeyshop.com/blog/en-us/zero-sievert-2-co-op-pve-zakov-news-r/)). **Read: the sequel bets on co-op and dynamic in-raid events as the fixes for solo repetition.**

### 3.2 Other top-down / 2D extraction shooters

The niche is thin — a curated 2026 list found only three clearly top-down/2D entries ([wasdland](https://www.wasdland.com/games/extraction-shooter/)):

- **Hell is Others** (Strelka/Yonder, Oct 2022) — top-down **PvPvE**, noir "Century City"; blood is currency, ammo grown from plants in your apartment; 10-minute raids ending at an elevator ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/i-cant-stop-arguing-with-my-bathroom-wall-in-hell-is-others-a-bizarre-top-down-extraction-shooter/), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/964440/Hell_is_Others/)). Praised: tension, art, audio-first threat detection (enemies hum/buzz, footsteps differ by surface) ([mp1st](https://mp1st.com/reviews/hell-is-others-review-next-stop-hell), [3rd-strike](https://3rd-strike.com/hell-is-others-review/)). Criticized: clunky movement, overly prescriptive progression (forces quest-rushing, punishes looting/fighting), and player-count collapse — all-time peak 937, later peaks ~21 ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/964440/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated)). **Lesson: top-down + PvP extraction = double niche; population death spiral.**
- **Quasimorph** (Magnum Scriptum, EA 2023) — top-down **turn-based** extraction RPG, proc-gen multi-floor space stations, 84% positive from ~3,268 reviews ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2059170/Quasimorph/), [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/quasimorph-impressions/)). Criticized for unfair difficulty spikes and downtime hunting for the elevator between floors ([TapTap](https://www.taptap.io/post/5883329), [rpgcodex](https://rpgcodex.net/forums/threads/quasimorph-dark-turn-based-extraction-rpg-now-available-on-early-access.147330/)).
- **Last Group Out** (Feb 2022) — casual 2D co-op extraction, "Tarkov meets The Escapists"; small/obscure ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1843730/)).
- **Blood Running** (1.0 ~2025) — cyberpunk pixel top-down extraction: randomly generated world, randomized loot layouts, 100+ items, base enhancement, merchants ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2641490/Blood_Running/)).
- Adjacent: **HOLE** (2024/25) proves ultra-small-scope single-player extraction works even as low-res FPS — 3 maps, praised gunplay, dinged for weak AI and thin content ([pixeldie](https://pixeldie.com/2025/03/12/hole-review/), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2971610/HOLE/)); **Gravebound** (EA Q1 2026) is third-person PvEvP with Borderlands visuals + bullet-hell chaos ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/third-person-shooter/extraction-shooter-gravebounds-bullet-hell-action-adds-an-exciting-twist-to-the-genre-and-you-can-try-it-out-in-an-open-play-test-right-now/)). "Games like ZERO Sievert" threads also name Road to Vostok, Incursion Red River (both 3D FPS) ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1782120/discussions/0/594025265594666753/), [gg.deals](https://gg.deals/game/zero-sievert/similar-games/)).

**Takeaway: single-player/co-op PvE top-down extraction has exactly one breakout (ZERO Sievert) plus its sequel; the space is far from saturated as of 2026.**

### 3.3 Vision/LOS, fog of war, roof cutaway — technical patterns

**Vision/FOV models (pick one deliberately):**
- **Visibility polygon (line-segment sweep):** cast rays to wall endpoints, sweep 360°, build a lit polygon; store in bitmap/stencil for lighting. Canonical writeup: [Red Blob Games: 2D Visibility](https://www.redblobgames.com/articles/visibility/) (JS port: [Silverwolf90/2d-visibility](https://github.com/Silverwolf90/2d-visibility)). This is the Monaco approach — whole level rendered as blueprint, live entities only inside the sight polygon ([Unity discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/how-to-make-a-top-down-field-of-view-similar-to-monaco/90582)).
- **Grid shadowcasting:** recursive shadowcasting is fastest on indoor maps, best all-round for tile worlds ([RogueBasin comparative study](https://www.roguebasin.com/index.php/Comparative_study_of_field_of_view_algorithms_for_2D_grid_based_worlds), [Bob Nystrom's walkthrough](https://journal.stuffwithstuff.com/2015/09/07/what-the-hero-sees/), [go-fov](https://github.com/norendren/go-fov)).
- **Nox (2000) "TrueSight":** the gold-standard precedent — dynamically blacks out unseen screen areas, computed from tile corners (sub-tile precise), updates when doors open/close ([Unity thread](https://forum.unity.com/threads/line-of-sight-replicating-as-in-nox-2000.809013/), [OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org/content/nox-line-sight)).
- **Teleglitch trick:** render tall black extruded walls above solid objects from a top-down camera — geometry itself occludes, no FOV algorithm needed ([indiegamereviewer](https://indiegamereviewer.com/review-teleglitch-a-fast-paced-arcade-style-rogue-like-yes-it-is/)).
- **Directional cone as tension (Darkwood):** clear vision only in a mouse-aimed cone; peripherals darkened; enemies outside the cone don't render but the static map does. Acid Wizard: "horror that stimulates imagination and lets minds fill in the blanks" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-darkwood-i-s-visibility-mechanics-create-a-new-kind-of-horror); imitations: [Godot](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-can-i-replicate-this-fog-of-war-vision-effect-from-darkwood/39305), [GDevelop](https://forum.gdevelop.io/t/vision-cone-like-in-darkwood-solved/60620)). ZERO Sievert uses the same model; its failure modes (cone through walls, enemy detection > player vision) are the cautionary tale ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/ZERO%20Sievert)).
- **Rule of thumb:** terrain/map stays visible (or greyed "remembered"); *entities* are strictly LOS-gated. Darkwood and Monaco both do this — preserves navigation while keeping threat information scarce.

**Roof cutaway / interiors:**
- **Flood-fill room detection:** flood-fill connected roofed tiles into "indoor areas," hide only that area's roof when the player stands in it ([GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/673689-how-to-mark-roof-for-showhide/5265116/)).
- **Doorway sensor toggle:** trigger at each doorway flips that building's roof layer ([GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/673689-how-to-mark-roof-for-showhide/5265116/)).
- **Raycast camera-to-player:** for stacked floors, hide every floor above the player's current one ([Unity discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/if-my-player-walks-into-a-3d-building-in-a-top-view-game-how-would-i-get-the-roof-or-rest-of-the-buildings-stories-to-become-invisible/163081), [Unity forum](https://forum.unity.com/threads/2d-topdown-hiding-floors-between-player-and-camera.292726/)).
- **LOS-driven cutaway (best-in-class): Project Zomboid** — walls/floors/trees between camera and character cut away according to what the character's LOS would actually reveal; Build 42 added a per-tile pseudo-3D depth buffer ("like a deck of cards dealt every frame") so cutaways and occlusion compose correctly ([Build 42 blog](https://projectzomboid.com/blog/news/2023/02/play-your-cardz-right/), [PZwiki](https://pzwiki.net/wiki/Isometric_Development)). Even PZ's range is contested — a mod extends cutaway to 20 tiles because zombies hid inside the player's legitimate sightline ([PeekAView](https://github.com/armakupub/PeekAView)). **Cutaway range and LOS range must match or players die to things they "should" have seen — the #1 fairness complaint in both PZ and ZERO Sievert.**
- Simplest viable pattern (Hotline Miami / many roguelikes): never render roofs; buildings are open-topped and fog/LOS does the concealment.

### 3.4 Vertical layers: basements, upper floors, Z-transitions

- **Project Zomboid (max fidelity, max cost):** renders vertical slices — per tile column, draw up to max map height; strict draw order floor → N/W walls → furniture; Build 42 raised height limits (32-floor discussion: [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/108600/discussions/0/4297067919526958205/)) and uses the depth-buffer card system ([iso revolution](https://projectzomboid.com/blog/news/2013/09/iso-revolution/), [Build 42 blog](https://projectzomboid.com/blog/news/2023/02/play-your-cardz-right/)).
- **Cataclysm: DDA:** true z-levels as stacked 2D grids (z=0 ground, +1 roofs, −1 basements); per-level JSON mapgen with matching stair placement ([CDDA mapgen docs](https://docs.cataclysmdda.org/JSON/JSON_Mapping_Guides/Guide_for_beginning_mapgen.html), [design discussion](https://discourse.cataclysmdda.org/t/z-levels/7695)). Lesson: z-levels ripple into AI, pathing, and NPC capability forever ([NPC z-level thread](https://discourse.cataclysmdda.org/t/npc-z-level-capabilities/11726)).
- **ZERO Sievert (cheap and effective):** no simultaneous vertical rendering. Underground spaces are separate maps (Sewers) or walled-off "underground" regions on the same 2D plane reached by stairs — Mall map contains the mall, basement garage, and parking lot as one 2D layout, with an extraction hidden underground ([TechRaptor](https://techraptor.net/gaming/guides/zero-sievert-zero-sievert-mall-map-and-locations-guide), [Fandom](https://zero-sievert.fandom.com/wiki/Mall), [Underground extraction](https://zero-sievert.fandom.com/wiki/Underground_extraction)).
- **Quasimorph:** floors as sequential levels via elevators; reviewers flag "wasted time trying to find the elevator to the next floor" as concrete downtime cost ([TapTap](https://www.taptap.io/post/5883329)).
- **Practical ranking:** (a) separate scenes/sub-maps per floor with stair transitions — cheapest, zero rendering ambiguity; (b) CDDA-style stacked grids if simulation needs it; (c) PZ-style simultaneous vertical rendering only if seeing between floors is a core fantasy. If floors are separate scenes, make stair/elevator locations legible or inherit Quasimorph's downtime complaint.

### 3.5 Genre-wide avoid list

1. **Repetition is the #1 complaint** — proc-gen alone doesn't fix it; "kill X 15 times" quests amplify it. ZS2's answer: weather, dynamic events, faction conflicts inside the raid.
2. **Vision fairness:** player vision, enemy detection range, and rendered information must be one consistent system.
3. **Aiming feel:** floaty cursors + punishing recoil vs pixel-accurate AI reads as cheating; handle multi-monitor cursor capture ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1782120/discussions/0/4032473829610445767/)); reworking gunfeel post-launch triggers revolt ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1782120/discussions/0/4034726433724520186/)).
4. **Darkness/light economy:** if lights are weak AND using them reveals you, night content is just worse content.
5. **Death-penalty tunability sells;** locking it at world-creation is the criticized part.
6. **Don't force one playstyle:** Hell is Others punished looting/fighting in favor of quest-rushing and got panned for it.
7. **PvP dependency kills small top-down games;** single-player/PvE-co-op is the proven lane (ZS, ZS2, HOLE).
8. **Audio is a top-down superpower:** directional enemy sound + surface-specific footsteps let players "see" off-screen — Hell is Others' most-praised system, and the fair answer to threats outside the vision cone.
9. **Onboarding:** hardcore-systems games get docked for weak teaching tools.

## 4. Unity netcode landscape and recommendation-relevant facts

### 4.1 Netcode for GameObjects (NGO)

- **Version:** 2.13.0 (June 22, 2026; 2.12.0 June 2, 2026; 2.11.2 May 1, 2026), actively maintained ([releases](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects/releases)); 2.x ships with Unity 6 ([docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.4/Documentation/Manual/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects.html)). Recent work is polish (NetworkBehaviour/NetworkTransform/NetworkRigidbody perf, Fast Enter Play Mode) ([changelog](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.11/changelog/CHANGELOG.html)).
- **Positioning:** built for small-scale co-op; **~64 players is the practical recommendation ceiling** (no hard technical limit) ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/maximum-players-connections-in-multiplayer-game-built-on-netcode/872966/21), [max-players docs](https://docs-multiplayer.unity3d.com/netcode/current/basics/max-players/index.html)). 12–20 players is in range with efficient sync code.
- **The big pain point: no built-in client-side prediction or lag compensation.** 2024–2026 threads document jitter, mispredictions, painful hand-rolled reconciliation; Unity's "Client Anticipation" API is widely reported as inadequate for shooter prediction ([1](https://discussions.unity.com/t/problems-with-client-side-prediction-in-netcode-for-game-objects/1673001), [2](https://discussions.unity.com/t/client-side-prediction-cycle-netcode-for-gameobjects/1710850), [3](https://discussions.unity.com/t/netcode-for-gameobjects-custom-client-prediction-tips/1535760), [4](https://discussions.unity.com/t/netcode-for-gameobjects-prediction-and-reconciliation/1575393)). Minor: connection-approval delegate returns only a boolean ([forum](https://forum.unity.com/threads/maximum-players-connections-in-multiplayer-game-built-on-netcode.1244812/)).
- **Distributed authority** explicitly warns: "not suitable for high-performance competitive games that require an accurate predictive motion model" and "it can be easier for bad actors to cheat"; Unity says competitive FPS-type games should use server authority ([distributed authority docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.11/manual/terms-concepts/distributed-authority.html), [authority docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.11/manual/terms-concepts/authority.html)).
- **Shipped proof for co-op:** Lethal Company runs on NGO ([lethal.wiki](https://lethal.wiki/dev/advanced/networking)).

### 4.2 FishNet and Mirror

**FishNet:**
- ~4.7.2 (April 2026), actively developed ([releases](https://github.com/FirstGearGames/FishNet/releases)). Free with **no CCU caps**; host anywhere including player-hosted ([docs](https://fish-networking.gitbook.io/docs)).
- Feature-richest free option: **built-in client-side prediction (free)**; Pro adds **lag compensation** (rollback colliders for hit-reg), network LOD, server-code stripping, NetworkTransform extrapolation. Pro pricing: $10 initial + $2/mo (GitHub/Patreon), $60 lifetime, or $25–50 on the Asset Store with lifetime updates ([Pro page](https://fish-networking.gitbook.io/docs/overview/readme/pro-projects-and-support), [Asset Store](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/tools/network/fishnet-pro-networking-evolved-287711)).
- Positive sentiment, responsive solo maintainer; acknowledged weakness is a **smaller tutorial/third-party ecosystem** than Mirror or NGO ([Ocean View Games comparison](https://oceanviewgames.co.uk/blog/posts/cross-platform-multiplayer-networking-unity), [Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/fishnet-vs-netcode-for-game-objects/928418)). Production use claimed across MMOs/shooters/BRs; SteamDB detected-games list: [steamdb.info/tech/SDK/FishNet](https://steamdb.info/tech/SDK/FishNet/).
- **"Server authoritative by design"** while permitting host mode for development ([docs](https://fish-networking.gitbook.io/docs)).

**Mirror:**
- v96.10.3 (June 27, 2026); MIT, free, UNET-derived API (huge legacy tutorial base), same-project client/server — often called simplest to learn ([github](https://github.com/MirrorNetworking/Mirror), [releases](https://github.com/MirrorNetworking/Mirror/releases)).
- Claims **1,000+ shipped Steam games, 200M+ players**; flagship Population: ONE; ~15k-developer Discord ([mirror-networking.com](https://mirror-networking.com/)). Biggest shipped-game track record of the three.
- Trade-off: FishNet gives finer sync control and generally better community-benchmark performance; Mirror has no mature built-in CSP/lag-comp comparable to FishNet Pro ([comparison](https://oceanviewgames.co.uk/blog/posts/cross-platform-multiplayer-networking-unity)).

**Consensus (2025 threads):** "all three work; NGO for UGS integration + official support, Mirror for simplicity + proven scale, FishNet for features/performance + prediction" ([comparison thread](https://discussions.unity.com/t/so-what-is-the-difference-between-netcode-for-gameobjects-mirror-and-fishnet-which-one-is-better-for-fast-co-op/1678432)). Independent benchmark repo exists but data is from 2023 ([StinkySteak/unity-netcode-benchmark](https://github.com/StinkySteak/unity-netcode-benchmark)).

### 4.3 Photon (Fusion / Quantum)

- **Fusion 2:** tick-based sim with first-class client-side prediction, snapshot interpolation, built-in lag compensation; three topologies (dedicated server, client-host with host migration, shared authority); claims 6x lower bandwidth than MLAPI/Mirror ([photonengine.com/fusion](https://www.photonengine.com/fusion)). Showcases: Among Us VR, Atomic Picnic. Technically the most "shooter-complete" off-the-shelf netcode.
- **Quantum:** fully deterministic rollback engine — Unity becomes a view layer; "like learning a new engine"; aimed at fighters/RTS — overkill for an extraction shooter ([Noble Steed Games](https://noblesteedgames.com/blog/photon-fusion-101/), [Crux comparison](https://crux.supercraft.host/blog/photon-real-time-networking-vs-backend-services/)).
- **Pricing (2026):** free **100 CCU** tier for Fusion and Quantum ([blog](https://blog.photonengine.com/new-free-100-ccu-for-photon-fusion-and-quantum-games/)); 200 CCU "Plus" $95/year ([blog](https://blog.photonengine.com/new-200-ccu-plus-package-100-paid-100-free/)); 500 CCU $125/mo; 2,000 CCU $500/mo; $0.50/CCU beyond; traffic allowances included, CCU-burst so players aren't cut off ([pricing](https://www.photonengine.com/fusion/pricing), [pricing blog](https://blog.photonengine.com/multiplayer-pricing-made-simple/)). 100 free CCU comfortably covers a niche indie EA (100 concurrent ≈ thousands of DAU).
- **Cons:** perpetual per-CCU opex scaling with success; vendor lock-in to Photon Cloud; networking-only — an extraction shooter still needs auth/persistence/economy (persistent stash backend) elsewhere ([Crux](https://crux.supercraft.host/blog/photon-real-time-networking-vs-backend-services/)).

### 4.4 Hosting models

**Listen server (player host) + Unity Relay:**
- Relay pricing: **free up to 50 average monthly CCU**, then $0.16/extra CCU + regional egress ([Unity support](https://support.unity.com/hc/en-us/articles/4410136449812-How-is-the-Relay-Service-Priced), [UGS pricing](https://unity.com/products/gaming-services/pricing), [CodeSmile calculator](https://codesmile.de/2024/07/17/unity-gaming-services-cost-calculator/)). Effectively $0 for a small co-op game.
- Cheating: the host machine **is** the authority — a cheating host manipulates anything undetectably; honest hosts get 0-ping advantage. Fine for friends-co-op; **not viable for open PvP extraction with loot/progression at stake** ([authority docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.netcode.gameobjects@2.11/manual/terms-concepts/authority.html)).

**Dedicated servers — 2026 landscape shake-up:**
- **Unity Multiplay/Game Server Hosting shut down March 31, 2026** (software licensed to Rocket Science Group, ex-Multiplay engineers; no published pricing yet). Relay, Lobby, Matchmaker, NGO unaffected ([Unity status notice](https://status.unity.com/info_notices/362941), [Gameye migration write-up](https://gameye.com/blog/unity-multiplay-shutdown-migration-options/)). **Hathora shut down May 5, 2026** after acquisition by Fireworks AI ([Gameye](https://gameye.com/blog/game-server-shake-up-2026/)). Survivors: **Edgegap** (615+ locations, Unity/Mirror/FishNet plugins), **Gameye**, **GameFabric (Nitrado/Agones)** ([gameye.com](https://gameye.com/best-game-server-hosting/)).
- **Edgegap cost (Q1 2026):** $0.00115/min per vCPU (≈$0.069/hr), fractionable to ¼ vCPU (≈$0.017/hr), + $0.10/GB egress; free tier, no credit card ([pricing](https://edgegap.com/resources/pricing)). Ballpark: one optimized 20-player match server at ½–1 vCPU ≈ $0.03–0.07/hour, only while matches run (scale-to-zero).
- Extraction-shooter precedent: Ghosts of Tabor (VR PvPvE, 6–20 players/raid) runs on Edgegap after mixed bare-metal/cloud proved costly ([case study](https://edgegap.com/case-studies/combat-waffle-studios-ghosts-of-tabor), [ghostsoftabor.com](https://ghostsoftabor.com/)).

**Migration path co-op → PvP:** NGO/FishNet/Mirror all support listen-server and headless dedicated builds from one codebase. Safe pattern: ship co-op on host+Relay at $0, but **write server-authoritative gameplay from day one** (never trust client for damage/loot/inventory); later swap transport/hosting to a Linux headless build on Edgegap-style orchestration + matchmaker. Edgegap ships one-click plugins for Mirror/FishNet/Unity ([Mirror plugin](https://edgegap.com/integration/mirror-networking-plugin-automated-game-server-hosting-unity-tutorial), [Unity plugin](https://edgegap.com/gaming/unity-game-server-hosting-orchestration-plugin)). Photon Fusion's path: client-host mode → dedicated-server mode, same API, but you keep paying CCU + hosting.

### 4.5 Solo-beginner learnability

- **NGO — deepest beginner pipeline:** official Unity Learn course ([learn.unity.com](https://learn.unity.com/tutorial/get-started-with-netcode-for-gameobjects)), Unity's multiplayer networking ebook ([unity.com/blog](https://unity.com/blog/multiplayer-networking-ebook)), Code Monkey's free ~10-hour complete multiplayer course (Kitchen Chaos) + free beginner course covering NetworkVariables, RPCs, lobbies, Relay ([course](https://unitycodemonkey.com/kitchenchaosmultiplayercourse.php), [video](https://unitycodemonkey.com/video.php?v=7glCsF9fv3s)). Broadest presence in docs/QA content LLMs are trained on.
- **Mirror:** enormous legacy tutorials (years of YouTube/Udemy, e.g., [Steam+Mirror FPS course](https://www.udemy.com/course/unity-multiplayer-fps-game-1v1/)), ~15k Discord, GitBook docs ([docs](https://mirror-networking.gitbook.io/docs)).
- **FishNet:** good GitBook docs, helpful Discord, measurably fewer tutorials — expect more doc-reading, less copy-follow ([comparison](https://oceanviewgames.co.uk/blog/posts/cross-platform-multiplayer-networking-unity)).
- **Photon:** strong official docs/samples ([doc.photonengine.com](https://doc.photonengine.com/photon/current/pricing)), but Fusion 2's tick/prediction model is conceptually harder for a first project; Quantum is a different paradigm entirely.

### 4.6 Decision-relevant synthesis

- **Co-op phase:** NGO + Relay/Lobby is the cheapest, best-tutorialized path, proven by Lethal Company-class games. Its ceiling risk appears exactly when you become a PvP shooter needing prediction + lag compensation, which NGO still lacks natively in 2026.
- **FishNet is the only free stack that already ships CSP (free) + lag compensation (cheap Pro)**, has no CCU costs, is server-authoritative by design, and has a clean listen-server→dedicated migration — at the cost of thinner tutorial/AI coverage.
- **Photon Fusion** buys shooter-grade netcode maturity immediately (prediction, lag comp, host migration) with 100 free CCU, but locks in CCU opex and Photon Cloud, plus a second vendor beside your server host.
- **12–20 players is inside every option's envelope.** Real differentiators: prediction/lag-comp, host-cheating, and hosting cost — and post-consolidation, Edgegap-style usage-billed orchestration is the default indie dedicated-server answer at ~$0.02–0.07/server-hour.

### 6.2 What killed the failed ones

- **The Cycle: Frontier (YAGER):** launched June 2022, 40k peak CCU, shut down Sept 2023 ("not financially viable"). Causes: cheater flood immediately at launch; anti-cheat fixes arrived after the exodus; seasonal content couldn't win players back; recovering lost players requires relaunch-scale marketing ([mmos.com](https://mmos.com/news/the-cycle-frontier-september-shutdown), [Medium postmortem](https://medium.com/@SimplyEdwardGG/shut-down-of-the-cycle-frontier-83bad9a75e7b)). **In PvP extraction, the anti-cheat window is the first ~8 weeks; you don't get a second chance.**
- **Marauders (Small Impact Games + Team17):** EA Oct 2022, mixed 69% reviews, now **2–17 concurrent players** (June 2026), "Mostly Negative" recent — the slow extraction-shooter death, live ([SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/1789480/charts/), [PlayerAuctions](https://www.playerauctions.com/player-count/marauders/)).
- **Spectre Divide — the death-spiral mechanism:** by the time early problems were fixed, population was too low for fast/fair matchmaking or meaningful revenue; bad first impressions → smaller pools → worse matches → more churn ([DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/dead-multiplayer-games/), [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/highguard-layoffs-dead-game-awards-live-service-2000668192)).
- **The Day Before (Fntastic):** small studio + unpaid "volunteers" attempting a survival "MMO"; constantly redefined scope (shipped as an extraction shooter, not the promised MMO); removed from sale, studio closed **4 days after launch** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Day_Before), [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/the-day-before-has-failed-financially-and-the-dev-is-closing-down-just-4-days-after-launch-we-dont-have-the-funding-to-continue-the-work/)).
- **Coregrounds (solo-dev multiplayer, 2018) — canonical solo-MP postmortem:** launched April 2018, shut down within the year. Solo multiplayer complexity underestimated despite warnings; a few dozen die-hard fans created a false sense of viability ([frgmnts.blog](https://frgmnts.blog/f/how-not-to-make-a-game.html)).
- **Even funded teams are failing in this genre:** Steel Hunters (Wargaming) dead months after EA; Ubisoft cancelled its WWII extraction project; Bungie's Marathon delayed indefinitely ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/another-live-service-shooter-to-shut-down-a-few-months-after-launch/1100-6533057/), [ixbt](https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/02/14/ubisoft-otmenila-extraction-suter-o-vtoroi-mirovoi-i-eshhe-neskolko-neanonsirovannyx-proektov-smi.html), [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/marathon-getting-delayed-need-talk-about-extraction-shooters/)).
- BattleBit's own conclusion after *succeeding*: "If you are planning to make a multiplayer game, don't! … Make a single player game" — be prepared to be "slammed by a hammer" ([gameworldobserver](https://gameworldobserver.com/2023/07/04/battlebit-remastered-sales-1-8-million-copies-case)).

### 6.3 Genre-specific scope traps

- **Economy balancing:** extraction economies are live systems needing wipes, sinks, and market-manipulation defenses (Tarkov's Found-in-Raid flags, flea-market taxes, periodic wipes) — ongoing live-ops work, not ship-once ([carrylord](https://carrylord.com/escape-from-tarkov/economy-guide/), [bittopup](https://news.bittopup.com/news/delta-force-extraction-vs-tarkov-is-operations-mode-worth-it-in-season-e-40530850)). Even tiny PvE games hit it: Incursion Red River's "economy collapses into irrelevance once you clear the mid-game hump" ([thegamerreport](https://thegamerreport.com/incursion-red-river-early-access-review-gunplay-ai-problems-and-broken-economy/)) — but in PvE a broken economy is a review complaint; in PvP it's an exodus.
- **Anti-cheat:** Easy Anti-Cheat is now free at any studio size, but integration/ban-ops/kernel-bypass arms-race work is not; commercial solutions historically $0–10k/month; indie MP games without adequate anti-cheat see "rapid community abandonment within weeks" ([easy.ac](https://www.easy.ac/), [unfairgaps](https://unfairgaps.com/us/video-game-industry/anti-cheat-and-fair-play-infrastructure-costs), [getgud.io](https://www.getgud.io/blog/the-4-best-anti-cheat-solutions-for-indie-games-in-2023/)). High-stakes loot loss makes extraction the genre where cheating hurts most.
- **Server costs:** dedicated servers ~$5–150+/month each, scaling to $1,000s; AWS GameLift markets "down to $1 per user per month" — ruinous at indie revenue if you need authoritative dedicated servers ([easypc.io](https://www.easypc.io/game-hosts/cost/), [AWS GameLift pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/gamelift/servers/pricing/)). Lobby-based P2P/relay co-op (Lethal Company model) sidesteps this entirely.
- **Population death spiral:** PvP extraction needs enough CCU per region/time-zone/skill-band to fill raids. Below threshold: long queues → lopsided lobbies → churn → lower CCU; games that dipped never recovered without relaunch-scale marketing (The Cycle, Spectre Divide, Marauders).
- **Genre-audience mismatch:** extraction demands high investment and punishes death with progress loss; miss one "unwritten rule" and the game struggles; analysts now doubt extraction is "the next battle royale" at all ([vocal.media](https://vocal.media/gamers/explaining-why-companies-can-t-make-a-good-extraction-shooter), [XDA](https://www.xda-developers.com/marathon-getting-delayed-need-talk-about-extraction-shooters/)).
- **Live-service content treadmill:** players assume seasonal cadence; The Cycle's Season 2 failed to move the needle; a solo dev cannot sustain season-scale content + economy tuning + anti-cheat ops.

### 6.4 Strategies that worked

- **PvE/single-player-first, multiplayer later (or never):** ZERO Sievert (SP-only, $1M week one), Road to Vostok (SP by explicit risk calculus), Sulfur (PvE-only, 4 devs, 50k fast). Gray Zone Warfare even at ~100 devs ships dedicated PvE-only servers as a first-class mode ([grayzonewarfare.com](https://www.grayzonewarfare.com/)). Single-player-Tarkov demand is proven and underserved.
- **Lobby-based co-op instead of matchmade PvP:** eliminates server costs, matchmaking-population risk, and most anti-cheat pressure. GameDiscoverCo data shows online co-op titles have unusually strong wishlist→sale conversion (PEAK, R.E.P.O., Mage Arena) ([gamedevreports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-the-state-of-steam)); Lethal Company is the ceiling case.
- **Years of public playtesting to build population before launch:** BattleBit ran free weekend playtests from 2016, growing 20–30 players → 100+ CCU → 800k wishlists over 7 years ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/behind-i-battlebit-remastered-s-i-seven-year-journey-to-become-a-smash-hit)); Dark and Darker ran 5 public playtests (100k+ CCU) before EA ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_and_Darker)).
- **Demos + devlogs + creators + publisher for the wishlist ramp:** ZERO Sievert: Kickstarter (€30k month one), YouTube devlogs, Reddit, YouTuber collabs, Steam page up ~1 year pre-launch, Modern Wolf for the final push ([gotestify](https://www.gotestify.com/resources/the-success-of-zero-sievert-or-how-a-one-dev-team-had-a-1-million-launch)). Sulfur: two demos (March + June) played by creators → 160k wishlists ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-sulfur-sold-50k-real-fast-and)).
- **Early Access is the norm** (Unturned 3 yrs EA, Valheim 5.5 yrs, ZERO Sievert 2 yrs) — but GameDiscoverCo risk data: underperformers averaged 411 days on Steam pre-release vs 214 for over-performers, and first-week review score (67% vs 91%) strongly predicts outcome — **launch EA only when the core loop is already fun** ([gamedevreports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamediscoverco-the-state-of-steam)).
- **Existing-audience leverage:** Unturned (Roblox Deadzone playerbase), Lethal Company (decade of Roblox games), BattleBit (recruited from the Ravenfield modding community).

### 6.5 Bottom line

- No true solo-dev **PvP extraction shooter** has shipped and survived; the closest solo successes (ZERO Sievert, Road to Vostok, Unturned, Lethal Company) all cut PvP, matchmaking, dedicated servers, or multiplayer entirely.
- Realistic timelines: solo single-player extraction ≈ 2–4 yrs to EA; 3-person multiplayer FPS ≈ 7 yrs part-time (BattleBit); 5-person co-op survival ≈ 4 yrs to EA, 9 to 1.0 (Valheim); a real Tarkov-like PvPvE took a 60–100-person veteran studio (Gray Zone Warfare).
- Consistent failure mode: launch with cheaters/bugs → population below matchmaking viability in weeks → seasonal content can't reverse it → shutdown within ~12–18 months.
- The de-risked path validated by the market: single-player/co-op PvE extraction loop first (proven demand, no servers, no anti-cheat arms race, economy tunable offline), lobby-based co-op as the multiplayer step, PvP only after a self-sustaining community exists — with a population-building runway of public playtests measured in years, not months.
